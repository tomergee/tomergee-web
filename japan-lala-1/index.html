🛂 Visas & Entry

Good news — no visa needed

USAIsrael Both U.S. and Israeli passport holders get visa-free entry to Japan for up to 90 days for tourism. Your trip is 10 days, so you're well within the limit — for all four travelers on either passport.

Before you fly: passports valid 6+ months past your return · complete Visit Japan Web a few days out for the immigration/customs QR codes · Japan's JESTA doesn't launch until 2028, so nothing extra to file.

⚠️ Two things to plan around

Mon, Nov 23 is a Japanese public holiday (Labor Thanksgiving) — making Sat 21 – Mon 23 a 3-day weekend. Kamakura on the 21st and Kyoto on the 23rd will be crowded: start early both days.

Both Ghibli sites need advance timed tickets. Ghibli Park releases Sept 10 (14:00 JST); Ghibli Museum releases Oct 10 (10:00 JST) via Lawson. Book a 10:00 entry slot for the Museum — the plan below is built around it.

🌇 The single most important planning fact: it gets dark at 4:30 PM

In late November Tokyo runs sunrise ~6:25, sunset ~16:30. That's only ten hours of daylight, and it shapes every day:

Before 16:30 — shrines, parks, gardens, temples, viewpoints, anything outdoors. Meiji Jingū actually closes at 16:10 in November (it opens and shuts with the sun), and Kamakura's temples close around 16:30 too.

After 16:30 — shopping, arcades, restaurants, and Tokyo's neon, which is when the city is at its best. Every day below front-loads the outdoor things and saves the lit-up things for the evening. Jet lag helps here: coming from LA you'll wake naturally around 5 AM for the first few days, so early starts are free.

📅 Tokyo, Hour by Hour

Day 1Wed, Nov 18 — LandingGinza

Don't plan anything. You'll have been travelling 15 hours and it's dark by the time you're out of the airport.

  • 15:55Land at Narita. Immigration and baggage take ~45 min — your Visit Japan Web QR codes speed this up.
  • 16:45Buy Suica / Welcome Suica cards for all four, then head in: Keisei Access Express direct to Shimbashi (no transfer), or the roomier Narita Express to Tokyo Station + two Yamanote stops.
  • 18:30Check in at the b ginza. Six-minute walk from Shimbashi.
  • 19:15Dinner in the Shimbashi izakaya alleys under the railway arches — cheap, loud, delicious, and thirty seconds from the hotel. Kids' win: or conveyor-belt sushi if they want something familiar.
  • 20:30Ten-minute walk up Chūō-dōri to see Ginza lit up, then a convenience-store run for water and breakfast bits. Sleep early — you'll be wide awake at 5 AM.
🛏️ the b ginza · 🌅 sunset was at 16:34, long before you landed
Day 2Thu, Nov 19 — Old TokyoTsukiji · Asakusa · Skytree

Everything today sits on one subway line, and jet lag gives you the best hours for free. Sensō-ji before the crowds, Skytree at sunset.

  • 06:45Awake early anyway — walk 10 minutes to Tsukiji Outer Market for breakfast. Grilled scallops, tamagoyaki on a stick, fresh sushi, strawberry daifuku. Busiest and best 7–10 AM. Let the girls pick from the stalls.
  • 08:30Toei Asakusa line, Higashi-Ginza → Asakusa — direct, ~15 min.
  • 09:00Sensō-ji while it's still quiet. The Kaminarimon lantern without a hundred people in your photo is worth the early start. Wander the grounds, draw an omikuji fortune.
  • 10:00Nakamise-dōri as the shops open — melon pan, ningyō-yaki, senbei, cheap yukata and folding fans.
  • 11:15Riverside walk (or one stop) to Kuramae — Tokyo's craft district.
  • 12:00Kakimori — design and bind your own notebook, choosing cover, paper and rings. The best souvenir of the trip. Check the day's opening hours — it opens late morning. Lunch in one of Kuramae's cafés after.
  • 14:30Over to Tokyo Skytree (one stop from Asakusa). Book a timed slot if you can.
  • 15:45☀ Go up for sunset at 16:34. You get the city in daylight, the sun going down over the Kantō plain, and then all of Tokyo switching its lights on. Best hour of the day up there.
  • 17:30Back to Ginza. Dinner + first proper Ginza wander — Itoya, Uniqlo and Muji flagships and the department stores run to ~20:00. The depachika food halls under Mitsukoshi and Matsuya discount their beautiful bentō after 19:00.
🛏️ the b ginza · Tsukiji is closed Sundays — Thursday is perfect
Day 3Fri, Nov 20 — Ghibli & the West SideKichijōji · Harajuku · Shibuya

Built around a 10:00 Museum slot. Meiji Jingū must happen before it closes at 16:10 — after that, everything is neon.

  • 08:40Ginza line to Shibuya, change to the Keiō Inokashira line to Kichijōji (~17 min).
  • 09:40Walk 15 minutes through Inokashira Park to the museum — the pond and maples are at peak colour in late November. A far nicer approach than the Mitaka side.
  • 10:00🎬 Ghibli Museum. Don't miss the exclusive short film (shown only here), the rooftop robot soldier, and the Catbus room. Allow 2–2.5 hours. No photos inside — put the phones away and just look.
  • 12:30Lunch in Kichijōji — try the Harmonica Yokochō alleys. It's a lovely neighbourhood: boutiques, its own Loft, and a famous menchi-katsu croquette stand worth queueing for.
  • 14:15Inokashira line back to Shibuya, one Yamanote stop to Harajuku.
  • 14:45☀ Meiji Jingū — must be before 16:10. A wide forest path through 100,000 donated trees, opening onto the great torii gate. A genuinely peaceful hour in the middle of the city.
  • 16:00Takeshita Street as the lights come on — crêpes, purikura photo booths, kawaii shops. Then Kiddyland, the Pokémon Center and Nintendo Tokyo. Peak daughter territory.
  • 17:30Walk Cat Street down to Shibuya (~15 min) — boutique-lined and lit, with Omotesandō's designer avenue one street over if the shopping mood strikes.
  • 18:15The Shibuya Scramble Crossing and Hachikō. Cross it twice — it's better at night.
  • 18:45Shibuya Sky — the open-air rooftop. Night slots are easier to book than sunset and the view of the city lit up is the one you'll remember. Then dinner in Shibuya and the Ginza line home.
🛏️ the b ginza · Drop Meiji Jingū if the museum runs long — everything else works after dark
Day 4Sat, Nov 21 — Kamakura & the SeaDay trip

Holiday-weekend Saturday, so go early. Ends with the Mt. Fuji view you gave up when we dropped Hakone.

  • 07:20Walk to Shimbashi, JR Yokosuka line direct to Kamakura — no transfers, ~55 min. Grab breakfast at the station.
  • 08:30Arrive Kamakura. Enoden two stops to Hase.
  • 08:50Great Buddha (Kōtoku-in) — opens 08:00, so you'll have the 13-metre bronze Amida almost to yourselves. For ¥50 you can climb inside him. Kids love that.
  • 09:50Hase-dera — hillside gardens, sea views, a cave of tiny carved statues, and maples at full colour. One of the loveliest spots in the region.
  • 11:30The Enoden — a little green tram that rattles between houses and then bursts out along the coastline. Ride it to Enoshima.
  • 12:00Seaside lunch. The local speciality is shirasu (whitebait) over rice. Then explore Enoshima island — shrine, sea caves, and the Sea Candle tower.
  • 15:30Enoden back a few stops to Shichirigahama or Kamakura-Kōkōmae — the famous beachside level crossing.
  • 16:34☀ Sunset over the bay. On clear winter afternoons Mt. Fuji stands in silhouette across the water from this stretch of coast. It's the single best free view of the trip — and it's exactly what Hakone would have given you.
  • 17:15Train back to Tokyo. Dinner in Ginza — Saturday evening is when it's liveliest.
🛏️ the b ginza · Temples close ~16:30 — do them in the morning

💡 Practical Notes

Getting around

Everyone gets an IC card (Suica/PASMO). Shimbashi is your hub — Yamanote, Ginza line, Yokosuka line, the airport-direct Asakusa line and the Yurikamome all meet there. Skip the JR Pass; point-to-point Shinkansen tickets are cheaper.

Working with the short days

Sunrise ~06:25, sunset ~16:30. Outdoor and temple things before 16:30; shopping, food and neon after. Jet lag makes 6 AM starts effortless for the first three days — use them.

Weather

Crisp and mostly sunny — roughly 7–16°C (45–61°F), colder by the sea in Kamakura. Layers, a warm jacket, and shoes you can walk 20,000 steps in. Peak autumn foliage.

With the girls

Build in snack breaks — the hotel's free morning snack (6:30–9:00) covers quick starts and convenience stores are everywhere. A coin purse each makes gachapon and souvenirs their own adventure.
